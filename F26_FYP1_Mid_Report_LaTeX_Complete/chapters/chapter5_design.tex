\chapter{System Design}

\section{System Architecture}
The BuildScan~3D application is constructed on a three-level client-server architecture.

\begin{enumerate}
    \item The client level is represented by a mobile app for Android, which is capable of capturing video with ARCore, uploading it to the server, and displaying the current progress of processing.
    \item The server layer contains a FastAPI backend handling job arrangements, orchestration of the pipeline, and handling RunPod workloads.
    \item At the last level of computing, the system performs GPU processing by either using the local computer having all the necessary programs installed or using a pod from RunPod service.
\end{enumerate}

In this system, the data is transmitted in a strict sequence: Capture $\rightarrow$ Upload $\rightarrow$ Process $\rightarrow$ Download. The server is acting as a controller managing the GPU and passing the information to the client through regular polling of the server. Figure~\ref{fig:architecture} visualizes how every level function and communicates.

\section{Architectural Components}
\label{sec:components}

\paragraph{Android App (\texttt{googleARCore/samples/hello\_ar\_java/})}
\begin{itemize}
    \item The \texttt{HelloArActivity} class is in charge of the AR camera, including the recording controls, the settings menu and the quality overlay.
    \item The \texttt{CaptureLogger} class is the background I/O executor which writes the poses, tracking, points, quality, depth and metadata files.
    \item The \texttt{QualityMonitor} class is responsible for the analysis of the camera frames in terms of blurry images, brightness, speed of motion, and texture.
    \item The \texttt{CoverageMap} class keeps track of the coverage by using voxels with 10\,cm cubes and maps directions with 8 azimuth $\times$ 2 elevation bins.
    \item The \texttt{CoverageOverlayView} class is responsible for the creation of red/yellow/green dots on the camera images.
    \item The \texttt{ProcessingActivity} class uploads and shows progress with the help of OkHttp.
\end{itemize}

Figure~\ref{fig:androidarch} illustrates the connection between these classes and screens.

\paragraph{Processing Server (\texttt{server/})}
\begin{itemize}
    \item \texttt{main.py}: FastAPI endpoints---\texttt{/upload}, \texttt{/status/\{job\_id\}}, \texttt{/jobs}, \texttt{/logs/\{job\_id\}}, \texttt{/retry/\{job\_id\}}, \texttt{/jobs/\{job\_id\}/cancel}, \texttt{/download/\{job\_id\}}, \texttt{/config}, \texttt{/gpus}, \texttt{/datasets}, \texttt{/reprocess}, \texttt{/run-on-pod}, \texttt{/health}.
    \item \texttt{pipeline.py}: Local pipeline runner with \texttt{Job} dataclass, \texttt{StepConfig} per-step timeouts, and subprocess execution with streaming stdout.
    \item \texttt{runpod\_client.py}: RunPod API client (GraphQL pod creation, SSH/SCP helpers, remote pipeline execution, pod watchdog, pod termination in \texttt{finally} blocks).
\end{itemize}

\paragraph{Reconstruction Pipeline (\texttt{src/arcore3dgs/})}
\begin{itemize}
    \item \texttt{fallback.py}: IMU-bridged tracking-gap recovery.
    \item \texttt{prepare.py}: Keyframe extraction from video (sharpest frame by Laplacian variance in each window of 8 frames).
    \item \texttt{select\_sharp.py}: Gyro-aware keyframe selection (least predicted motion blur per 0.2\,s window). Used for the v4--v6 models; not yet part of the one-command pipeline.
    \item \texttt{sfm\_colmap.py}: COLMAP SIFT matching + global mapper + lens self-calibration + Sim3 alignment to ARCore metric frame.
    \item \texttt{export\_nerfstudio.py}: Convert COLMAP output to Nerfstudio data format.
    \item Nerfstudio \texttt{ns-train splatfacto}: 3DGS training (30k iterations).
    \item Nerfstudio \texttt{ns-export}: Export \texttt{splat.ply}.
\end{itemize}

\section{Architecture Decisions and Rationale}

\begin{enumerate}
    \item \textbf{Using COLMAP SfM over ARCore poses for training}: Raw ARCore poses have a 2.32\,px median reprojection error (with slow drift); however, using bundle adjustment, the error is reduced to 0.62\,px. However, ARCore can re-anchor poses without notifying us (for example, poses before 60 seconds were drifting by 1\,m and 28\textdegree{}). ARCore also reports no lens distortion. COLMAP's self-calibrated SfM with lens distortion (k1 $\approx$ 0.09 and k2 $\approx$ -0.22) removes both issues. On the same recording, switching to COLMAP poses made our training approach vastly improve the training-view fit from 21.9\,dB to 28\,dB and increased the held-out value of SSIM from 0.78 to 0.86.

    \item \textbf{Using Splatfacto over custom training}: After it became evident that the custom training recipe produces degenerate splats, we opted for Nerfstudio's proven splatfacto implementation. In another room (\texttt{roomAttemptSadness2}), we achieved a score of 27.81\,dB held-out using splatfacto compared to 24.70\,dB of the corrected custom trainer.

    \item \textbf{Using RunPod on-demand over serverless}: Our on-demand pods enable fully controlled SSH pipeline, and are suited for long processing times (approximately 75 mins in total on RTX 3070).

    \item \textbf{HTTP polling over WebSockets}: Simpler implementation, works across firewalls/proxies, and 3-second polling interval is sufficient for a pipeline that takes minutes.
\end{enumerate}

\section{Design Evolution}
\begin{enumerate}
    \item \textbf{Proposal $\rightarrow$ Mid}: Live IQA and coverage guidance were added (absent from the original proposal). The training switched to COLMAP SfM after realization of ARCore pose accuracy issue. Meanwhile, the GPU bundle adjustment, Metric3D depth, stereo matcher, depth supervision, floater pruning, median filtering, and post-hoc flat-forcing were all tried but shelved by the team (Section~\ref{sec:parked}).

    \item \textbf{Custom trainer $\rightarrow$ splatfacto}: After two weeks of analysis resulting in identifying six root-cause issues of the splat degeneracy, training switched to Nerfstudio splatfacto. The custom preprocessing pipeline was preserved and verified.

    \item \textbf{Local-only $\rightarrow$ local + cloud}: RunPod cloud GPU support was added allowing the usage of machines without GPU and achieving speed with GPUs more powerful than the development machine generally used (RTX 4090 as the default one vs RTX 3070, with 8\,GB of memory).
\end{enumerate}

\section{Domain Model}
\label{sec:domain}
The fundamental entities of the domain comprise the following:
\begin{itemize}
    \item \textbf{Scan}: The session of recording that generates a dataset (video and sensor files).
    \item \textbf{Dataset}: A set of files from one recorded session (video, poses, depth, quality and meta information).
    \item \textbf{Job}: A process performed along with a status (queued, processing, completed and failed) and its current status in progress and output location. Evolution of the job is illustrated in Figure~\ref{fig:jobstate}.
    \item \textbf{Model}:  The 3D Gaussian Splatting model (\texttt{splat.ply}).
    \item \textbf{Measurement}: A distance measured in the metric model (FYP-2).
\end{itemize}

\section{Data Design}

\paragraph{Capture data (per scan):}
\begin{itemize}
    \item \texttt{dataset.mp4}: H.264 video, 1920$\times$1080, 30\,FPS.
    \item \texttt{poses.csv}: timestamp, tx, ty, tz, qx, qy, qz, qw, fx, fy, cx, cy (per frame).
    \item \texttt{tracking.csv}: timestamp, tracking\_state, failure\_reason (per frame).
    \item \texttt{points.csv}: timestamp, point\_id, x, y, z, confidence (ARCore feature points).
    \item \texttt{quality.csv}: timestamp, sharpness, brightness, motion\_speed, texture\_level (per analyzed frame).
    \item \texttt{depth/}: uint16 depth maps (mm) + uint8 confidence maps at 5\,Hz.
    \item \texttt{meta.json}: device model, camera config, resolution, ARCore version.
\end{itemize}

\paragraph{Pipeline intermediate data:}
\begin{itemize}
    \item \texttt{poses\_fused.csv}, \texttt{fallback\_report.json}: poses with tracking gaps bridged by the IMU.
    \item \texttt{work/images/}, \texttt{work/frames.json}: extracted keyframes (JPEG) and their ARCore poses.
    \item \texttt{work\_sfm/}: COLMAP database, sparse model, undistorted images, Sim3-aligned \texttt{frames.json}, \texttt{init\_points.ply}.
    \item \texttt{work\_sfm/transforms.json}: Nerfstudio-format camera transforms.
    \item \texttt{work\_sfm/gs\_sfm/splat.ply}: Final 3DGS model.
\end{itemize}

Figure~\ref{fig:dataflow} traces the capture files through the six pipeline steps. \texttt{quality.csv}, \texttt{meta.json} and \texttt{depth/} are uploaded and stored with the dataset, but the current six steps do not read them (keyframe extraction runs with \texttt{--depth 0}).

\section{Process / Behavior Design}
\label{sec:process}

The procedure for the end-to-end pipeline is as follows:

\begin{enumerate}
    \item \textbf{Fallback} (2-minute timeout): Use the readings from the gyroscope and accelerometer datasets taken from the \texttt{dataset.mp4} file. The tracking gaps from \texttt{tracking.csv} will be identified and bridged with IMU integration anchored at both ends.
    \item \textbf{Prepare} (5-minute timeout): Process the given clip, storing the sharpest frame from total 8 frames in terms of the Laplacian variance appearing in the relevant window under script \texttt{prepare.py} file. The gyro-aware selection of \texttt{select\_sharp.py} that produced v6 is not yet applied here.
    \item \textbf{SfM} (40-minute timeout): COLMAP will be used to perform feature extraction (using the SIFT algorithm), matching of the features, mapping, lens self-calibration, undistorting images, and aligning to the ARCore.
    \item \textbf{Exporting} (2-minute timeout): It is necessary to convert the result from COLMAP into Nerfstudio \texttt{transforms.json} format.
    \item \textbf{Train} (60-minute timeout): Train the Nerfstudio splatfacto model for 30,000 iterations with bilateral-grid exposure compensation, scale regularization and fixed poses.
    \item \textbf{Exporting the model} (5-minute timeout): It is the final stage to create \texttt{splat.ply} model.
\end{enumerate}

Figure~\ref{fig:pipelinesteps} depicts the above mentioned steps with the various timeframes taken during each stage.

Every subprocess captures the streaming stdout of the executed step. In case of timeout lapse, the process dies and the job is marked as failed. In RunPod mode, whenever a pod was previously created, it is killed in a \texttt{finally} block irrespective of the success of the step. Additionally, the pod is terminated by the watchdog thread when \texttt{MAX\_POD\_LIFETIME} (default value is 7200\,s) is reached. Figure~\ref{fig:sequence} shows the upload $\rightarrow$ process $\rightarrow$ download interaction in RunPod mode.
